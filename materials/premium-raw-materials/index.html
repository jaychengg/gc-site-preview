Materials & Finishes/Premium Raw Materials

Only the Best Are Built to Last.

In brief

Hardware is only as good as the metal it starts from. We choose high-grade brass and stainless steel before anything else is decided.

A finish can hide a weak material for a season, not for a decade. Porosity shows through the polish. A poor brass gives up its zinc in a wet bathroom. The wrong steel stains outdoors. So the material comes first on every project, chosen for how the part will be formed, machined, finished and used. It costs more at the start. It is why the product still works, and still looks right, years after it is installed.

What the Metal Has to Survive.

Every process tests the metal, and so does every year in service. Before we recommend a material we ask how the part will be made and where it will live.

  • Forming — how the alloy fills a forging die or an investment-casting mould.
  • Machining — how cleanly it cuts, and how well it holds a dimension afterwards.
  • Polishing — porosity and inclusions that are invisible in a rough part appear on a polished face.
  • Plating — the base metal and its surface decide how a finish adheres and how it looks.
  • Use — moisture, cleaning, handling and load over years of service.

Two Metals. No Shortcuts.

A

Brass

For detailed, polished and plated components.

  • Forges and machines cleanly, which suits hinge bodies, clamps and other parts with fine detail.
  • Takes a high polish and gives plated finishes a sound, even base.
  • In some water and humid conditions, zinc can be drawn out of certain brasses over time, leaving a weaker, copper-rich structure. This is dezincification, and alloy composition strongly affects how susceptible a brass is.
  • We select brass alloys engineered to resist dezincification, supported by verified material grades and test data where applicable.

B

Stainless Steel

For strength and a natural metal surface.

  • Used for railing components, structural glass fittings, handles and other parts where strength and a solid metal surface are required.
  • No single grade answers every setting. An interior handrail, a shower enclosure and an exposed balcony are different briefs.
  • Surface condition matters as much as grade: a smoother, well-finished surface holds less dirt and moisture.
  • Grade and finish are proposed together, for the environment you describe to us.

Technical notes

Details and Limits.

Specific values are confirmed per product in the quotation or on the approved drawing. We would rather state a limit than imply a promise.

Materials
Brass and stainless steel. The specific alloy or grade is confirmed per product and stated in the quotation or on the approved drawing.
Dezincification
Resistance depends on the alloy, the manufacturing process, water chemistry, temperature and time. We do not describe any brass as immune to dezincification. If your market requires a recognised dezincification-resistant grade or a specific test, state it in the enquiry.
Stainless steel and environment
No single grade is suitable for every interior, exterior, coastal and pool environment. See “Weather Is Part of the Design” for how we approach the application environment.
Documentation
Tell us at enquiry stage which material documentation your project or market requires, so that it can be addressed in the quotation.
Care
All metals in service need cleaning appropriate to the material and finish. Ask us for care guidance for the product you are specifying.
